# Outside material — what the pipeline keeps, and how

Two spreadsheets the founder handed over on 2026-09-22 with "keep those in
your algorithm": `joan_rivers_authentic_scenes-v2.xlsx` (20 televised scenes,
verbatim quotes, a technique column) and `matt_rife_verbatim_exchanges.xlsx`
(20 crowd-work exchanges, 15 one-liners, a mechanism column).

They are kept the way §7b keeps the 50 outside reactions: **mined for
mechanics, never shipped as lines.** Three reasons, all in the spec:

1. The lines are other people's material. Verbatim lines in `{{EXAMPLES}}`
   or the hall of fame would be reproduced, and Guardrail D exists to stop
   exactly that.
2. Most of them break NEVER WRITE: named real people (Loren, Kidman, Streep,
   Paltrow, Griffin, Hilton, Lawrence), bodies, age, anatomy, disability,
   race, the illness as prop (the implant, the amputee, the bear). The spec
   says the ridicule is for what they DID; this material is largely for what
   they ARE.
3. Prompt text is the founder's, verbatim. Nothing below is in a prompt.
   Where a mechanic is already a named move, it is mapped; where it is not,
   it is a candidate for the founder's next spec edit, not mine.

## What this changes in the algorithm

Nothing in prompt text. The two sheets confirm, from outside the house, the
moves the founder has already encoded — the object over-specified, the number
returned one notch more precise, the excuse answered literally, the domain
relocated — and they confirm the ban list from the other side: every line in
them that the spec would refuse is refused for a reason the spec already
states. The one candidate worth the founder's next edit: Rivers's scene 7
shape, warmth answered with a smaller, more administrative consequence, is
DEFLATE DOWNWARD applied to a compliment, and the spec has no exemplar for a
kind-adult spill in that shape.

The sheets themselves stay out of the repo; this ledger is the record.
